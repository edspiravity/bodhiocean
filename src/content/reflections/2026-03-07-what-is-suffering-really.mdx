Suffering, as usually experienced,<br />
is the feeling that arises<br />
when expectations meet reality —<br />
and reality does not comply.

What is desired did not happen.<br />
What is undesired keeps happening.<br />
The beauty did not last.<br />
The good did not last.<br />
The dread did not end.<br />
The mindless work did not stop.

Suffering is not merely pain.<br />
It is the instability of things.<br />
The constant change we face —<br />
stirring emotional and mental unrest,<br />
spinning us out of control.

We anticipate.<br />
We become excited.<br />
We prepare defences.<br />
We plan continuation.<br />
We live in the past.<br />

All in an attempt<br />
to secure what cannot be secured.

We hold tightly to the external world<br />
so that we may hold tightly<br />
to our internal world —<br />
feelings,<br />
the deeper mental constructs,<br />
and all that defines a “me”.

Perhaps suffering is not only that things change.<br />
Perhaps it is that we expect them not to.<br />

If what we cling to must shift,<br />
what then is truly stable?

## Reflection

When something pleasant ends,<br />
is there a subtle tightening?

When something unpleasant continues,<br />
is there a quiet resistance?

When plans fall apart,<br />
does the mind quickly begin to rewrite the world?

Suffering often does not announce itself as tragedy.<br />
It appears as restlessness.<br />
As irritation.<br />
As a faint dissatisfaction <br />
that hums beneath the day.

We say we want happiness.<br />
But what we often want<br />
is for things to remain as we prefer.

Yet nothing remains.

Not the joy.<br />
Not the ache.<br />
Not the body.<br />
Not the mood.

If this is so,<br />
what exactly are we struggling against?

And if everything shifts,<br />
what is it that insists it should not?

Let's sit quietly with that.

Not to answer it.<br />
But to see it.

Perhaps suffering is not something happening to us.<br />
Perhaps it is something we are holding on to.

## BodhiOcean

We are not here to accumulate ideas.<br />
We are here to loosen what is unnecessary.<br />
The ocean is already vast.<br />
We are only learning not to cling to the shore.
