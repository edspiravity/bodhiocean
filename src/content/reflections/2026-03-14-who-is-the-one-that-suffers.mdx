title: "Who Is The One That Suffers?"
date: "2026-03-014"

When something bad happens,<br />
I am angry, sad, and I suffer.

When something good happens,<br />
I am pleased, happy, and content.

These feelings come and go,<br />
changing from moment to moment.

Where am I?

When I grew from an infant to a youth,<br />
and from youth to an adult,

and from an adult to an elder,

and from an elder to the moment<br />
when this body returns to the earth —

Where am I?

Through this life of constant change,<br />
where do I stand?

And if there truly is an I,<br />
are these what I truly wanted?

Would I have chosen to suffer?

Why does the Buddha keep asking,<br />
“Where are you?”

Why did He say<br />
there is no self, no me, no I?

What did He mean when He said<br />
there is suffering,<br />
but no sufferer?

I always thought<br />
that everything happening around me<br />
was being experienced by an I.

But when I follow the Buddha’s questions<br />
and look carefully —

where is this “I”?

Inside the body?<br />
Outside the body?<br />
Somewhere in between?

When I search for it,<br />
I cannot find<br />
a separate entity called me.

## Reflection

The next time a strong feeling arises —<br />
be it anger, sadness, joy, or excitement —

pause for a moment.

Instead of following the story of the feeling,<br />
gently ask:

Who is the one experiencing this?

Is the one who is angry<br />
the same one who was happy a moment ago?

When the feeling fades,<br />
where does that “someone” go?

Look carefully.

Is the self located in the body?<br />
In the thoughts?<br />
In the emotions?

Or do these simply arise and pass,<br />
while the mind habitually calls them “me”?

Do not rush to answer.

Just observe quietly.

Perhaps the question itself<br />
opens a different way of seeing.

## BodhiOcean

We are not here to accumulate ideas.<br />
We are here to loosen what is unnecessary.<br />
The ocean is already vast.<br />
We are only learning not to cling to the shore.
